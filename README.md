# Describe Your Ex in One Word

Entry for the make.fun bounty
[Describe Your Ex in One Word](https://make.fun/bounty/u-puan-describe-your-ex-in-one-word-9fc6ebe7)
($2): sum up an ex in exactly one word, at most 111 characters — the
funniest one-word verdict wins, judged head to head by an AI judge.

## The entry

> unsubscribed

12 characters (gate: 1–111).

## Why this word should win

- **Instantly a verdict on an ex:** the word only makes sense as
  something you did *to* someone who kept showing up — you opted out
  of them. No caption needed; the relationship is the subscription.
- **Comic punch through a frame shift:** a person recast as inbox
  spam. The breakup becomes the one click that stops the daily
  messages — a twist, not a plain insult.
- **Surprise:** the field will be full of "toxic", "cheater" and
  "ghost". Nobody expects the breakup to be filed under email
  housekeeping, yet it is clear the second you read it.
- **Craft:** exactly one real word, spelled correctly, no punctuation
  tricks, no hyphen, no number. Twelve letters, and the joke arrives
  with the word itself.
- **Recognition:** everyone has unsubscribed from something annoying
  and felt the relief — and everyone has wished a person came with
  that button. The laugh comes with a nod.

## Alternatives considered

Seven words were drafted before this one was picked:

- "expired" (7 chars) — the ex as a coupon past its date. Clean, but
  flat: it states a fact and stops, with no twist.
- "archived" (8 chars) — filed away, out of sight. Quiet and a little
  sad; the joke whispers instead of landing.
- "recalled" (8 chars) — a defective product pulled back by the
  manufacturer. Strong, but it reads as a verdict on a *product*,
  not a person.
- "deprecated" (10 chars) — the dev-flavoured version: still around,
  officially no longer supported. Clever, but it needs tech knowledge
  to land, so recognition is narrower.
- "ghosted" (7 chars) — describes what the ex *did*, not the ex, and
  it is the obvious choice the judge is told to discount.
- "refund" (6 chars) — implies you got something back; the joke
  points the wrong way, at you rather than at them.
- "seasonal" (8 chars) — the ex as a limited-time offer. Amusing for
  a second, then it fades; no clear verdict in it.

The chosen word won because it stacks all five judge criteria in one
word: a verdict first, a twist second, and a feeling everyone already
knows.

## How it works

`main.py` writes the word to `/output/entry.txt`. It is fully
deterministic: the same bytes on every run, no input, no randomness, no
network. Standard library only.
